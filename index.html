Hello, I'm Felicia.

I'm studying data science at Midlands State University, after three years working in IT.

I started with a National Certificate and then a National Diploma in Information Technology at Gweru Polytechnic College. In 2024 I worked as an IT support intern at Midlands State University's Zvishavane campus, which meant hardware, software, networking and VoIP phone setup. It taught me to listen to what people actually describe, work through problems in order, and not stop until the thing works.

I moved into data science because I wanted to work with the information those systems hold, not just keep them running. I'm now in a Bachelor of Commerce in Data Science.

My main project is Vision-SL, a computer-vision prototype for Zimbabwean Sign Language. I'm also president of the MSU chapter of Data Science Zimbabwe, which is mostly about getting students in a room to learn together.

03 / Work

Projects.

What I've built so far, and what I'm working on next.

Main project

Vision-SL

An experimental computer-vision prototype exploring how Zimbabwean Sign Language signs can be introduced using as few as three example photographs.

  • Python
  • Computer vision
  • Few-example learning
  • Accessible technology

Problem

There isn't a dataset to start from.

There are very few public datasets of Zimbabwean Sign Language, which makes experimentation in this area difficult. Before anyone can test an idea, they first have to answer where the examples will come from.

Goals

What I set out to do.

  • Explore few-example learning approaches.
  • Experiment with computer vision.
  • Look into opportunities for accessible language technology.
  • Show how technology can address locally relevant problems.

How I built it

From question to prototype.

  1. Problem identification
  2. Research and exploration
  3. Dataset and image preparation
  4. Prototype development
  5. Testing and experimentation
  6. Evaluation and future improvements

My contribution

What I did.

I conceptualised Vision-SL and developed the prototype in Python. The project was shaped by guidance from Ms Marandure and Mr Rwakonda at the Midlands State University National Language Institute, whose expertise in language kept the work grounded in how the language is actually learned.

The prototype was prepared for Enovate4Impact 2026 in the STEM Innovation stream.
